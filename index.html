Render your room in Blender

Two files were saved to your Downloads folder: panel-planner-room.glb (the room) and panel-planner-blender.py (sets up the camera, sky, lights and render settings). Keep them in the same folder. Your browser may ask once to allow multiple downloads.

  1. Install Blender (free) from blender.org, version 4.2 or newer for the most accurate colours (older versions from 3.0 work, with a slightly washed-out look). On a Mac, drag it into Applications. To check your version: Blender > About Blender (Mac) or Help > About Blender.
  2. Open Blender and pick General on the splash screen (or File > New > General).
  3. Click the Scripting tab along the top of the window.
  4. In the text editor (the big empty panel in the middle), click Open and choose panel-planner-blender.py from your Downloads folder.
  5. Click Run Script (the ▶ play button at the top of the text editor). It takes a few seconds: it imports the room, downloads the sky photo (about 5 MB, the first time only) and sets everything up. If nothing seems to happen, open Window > Toggle System Console (Windows) to see messages.
  6. Press F12 (on a Mac laptop, fn + F12), or choose Render > Render Image. A window opens and the picture sharpens as it renders: about 2 to 10 minutes on a recent computer.
  7. In the render window choose Image > Save As to save it as a PNG or JPEG.

Faster renders: Edit > Preferences > System > Cycles Render Devices: choose Metal (Mac), OptiX or CUDA (NVIDIA) or HIP (AMD) and tick your graphics card. The script tries to switch this on for you. Cleaner or quicker: Render properties (camera-back icon on the right) > Sampling > Max Samples, 1024 by default.

Change the shot: set it up in the app (Design tab, Walk, Lens) and export again. In Blender you can also press Numpad 0 to look through the camera and move it with the View > Navigation tools.

Blender adds what the app can't: real reflections and gloss, soft accurate shadows from the sky, and a professional denoiser. You can also add furniture there.
